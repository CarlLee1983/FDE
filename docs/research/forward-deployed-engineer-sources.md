# Forward Deployed Engineer — Primary-Source Record

**Purpose.** This memo records what the organisations that own the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) title actually say about it, in their own job descriptions, blogs, docs, filings, and announcements. It establishes how Palantir, Anthropic, and OpenAI each define the role and describe the engagement it sits in. It does **not** claim that any of these definitions applies to this repository, does not propose adopting any of these practices, and does not expand or alter the capability boundary set by `CONTEXT.md` and `docs/adr/`. Job postings are snapshots that expire; every claim below is tagged **verified** (read at the cited URL during this run) or **inferred** (this author's reading across sources).

## Source facts

### 1. Palantir's own definition of the role

Palantir states in its live job description that it originated the title: "At Palantir, the Forward Deployed Software Engineer (FDSE) role isn't just a job title: it's the blueprint. We pioneered this unique position, embedding talented engineers directly with our customers to tackle their most pressing challenges head-on." The same posting defines the work as "architecting and building solutions that leverage business-critical data" and lists core responsibilities as architecture and design collaboration, "wrangling massive-scale data," "developing custom applications tailored to customer needs," "engaging directly with customer stakeholders, from technical teams to executives," and "shaping team strategy and driving projects from ideation to deployment." Requirements are notably modest: "1+ years of relevant, post-college work experience," strong coding in Python/Java/C++/TypeScript "or similar," and "ability and interest to travel up to 25%." Stated salary range is $135,000–$200,000/year plus RSUs. **verified** — [Palantir, Forward Deployed Software Engineer, New York](https://jobs.lever.co/palantir/dab396d4-2f14-4796-aac0-0d82883dccf0). **Retrieved:** 2026-08-29.

The sharpest statement of the capability boundary is Palantir's own engineering blog. It distinguishes the platform engineer ("Dev") from the forward-deployed engineer ("Delta"): "Devs (Software Engineers) develop and engineer our software platforms… Deltas (Forward Deployed Software Engineers) deploy our software platforms to customers… You can think of a Dev's focus as 'one capability, many customers,' while a Delta's focus is 'one customer, many capabilities.'" The post also states that Deltas sit organisationally in Business Development and that "their mandate is to achieve technical outcomes for our customers… They measure success in terms of impact on the customer's goal." **verified** — [Palantir, "Dev versus Delta: Demystifying engineering roles at Palantir", 2019-04-09](https://blog.palantir.com/dev-versus-delta-demystifying-engineering-roles-at-palantir-ad44c2a6e87).

Both sources explicitly reject the consultant framing. The Dev-versus-Delta post quotes a named practitioner: "consultants generally create a one-time analysis, recommendation, or solution to a specific problem, while at Palantir we work together with our customers to build a long-term solution that allows the customer to continuously improve themselves." An FDSE interviewed in a companion post says the differentiator is that "we can pull most of the pieces together out-of-the-box, meaning we don't need to reinvent the wheel for each customer." **verified** — [Palantir, "A Day in the Life of a Palantir Forward Deployed Software Engineer", 2020-11-02](https://blog.palantir.com/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-palantir-forward-deployed-software-engineer-45ef2de257b1).

**Reading.** Palantir's FDE is defined by *direction of leverage*, not by seniority or by a distinct engineering discipline: the platform already exists, and the engineer's job is to configure and extend it against one customer's operating problem. The 1+ year experience floor is consistent with that: the leverage comes from the platform, not from the individual's tenure. **inferred**, resting on the two blog posts and the JD read together.

### 2. Palantir's build/operate boundary, and the roles either side of it

The FDSE interview is explicit that operating responsibility does not stop at delivery: "I often engage in engineering reviews, code reviews, deployability optimization, maintenance and monitoring of production systems." One of the listed technical challenges is "How do I investigate a production software outage, identify a root cause of the issue, deploy a fix, and monitor the stack for stability while coordinating communication between product teams, our deployment team, and the customer?" **verified** — same 2020-11-02 post as above.

Palantir nevertheless staffs the operate side separately. The Forward Deployed Enablement Engineer posting describes engineers "embedded with centralized customer success teams to maximize the outcomes of our deployed products and workflows," responsible for triaging support requests, unblocking customer engineers, and participating in "a 24/7 on-call rotation responsible for coordinating responses to critical customer-facing incidents." **verified** — [Palantir, Forward Deployed Enablement Engineer – Customer Success, New York](https://jobs.lever.co/palantir/4cba9c95-d16f-440d-83e7-2352480f689f). **Retrieved:** 2026-08-29.

The non-engineering counterpart is the Deployment Strategist, whose posting describes going "onsite and meet[ing] with customer analysts to understand the critical questions they need to answer and locate their biggest problems," identifying relevant datasets, then "work[ing] with Forward Deployed Engineers to integrate the data into a stable and extensible pipeline" and leading user training. Required travel is "25 – 75%." **verified** — [Palantir, Deployment Strategist, New York](https://jobs.lever.co/palantir/e0ab8226-b928-4e3a-bf87-08fe7b1ea595). **Retrieved:** 2026-08-29.

### 3. The scale and internal differentiation of the Palantir role family

Palantir's public Lever job feed carried 307 open postings on the retrieval date, of which 68 have a title beginning "Forward Deployed": 50 Forward Deployed Software Engineer, 7 Forward Deployed Infrastructure Engineer, 3 Forward Deployed Enablement Engineer, 2 Forward Deployed AI Engineer, 2 Forward Deployed Reliability Engineer, and one each of Forward Deployed Engineer, Forward Deployed Security Engineer, and Forward Deployed Site Reliability Engineer. **verified** — counted directly from [Palantir's Lever postings API](https://api.lever.co/v0/postings/palantir?mode=json). **Retrieved:** 2026-08-29. This is a snapshot of one hiring day and carries no trend claim.

The Forward Deployed AI Engineer variant is described in terms that sit closer to this repository's subject matter: "Forward Deployed AI Engineers work directly with customers owning Gen AI strategy and implementation. On a daily basis, you will build end-to-end workflows, take them to production, and solve real world problems at the largest scale." Its stated values include "Solving real business problems, not academic benchmarks," and it requires "past experience building solutions with LLMs" plus "a strong foundation in Machine Learning basics (Evaluation, Training, Problem Decomposition)." Salary range is the same $135,000–$200,000 as the FDSE posting. **verified** — [Palantir, Forward Deployed AI Engineer, New York](https://jobs.lever.co/palantir/636fc05c-d348-4a06-be51-597cb9e07488). **Retrieved:** 2026-08-29.

**Vocabulary warning.** Palantir now also uses "AI FDE" as a *product* name, not a role: its Foundry documentation defines "AI FDE, the AI-powered forward deployed engineer" as "an interactive agent that operates Foundry for you through conversational commands," which edits ontologies, writes functions, audits permissions, and proposes changes on a branch for review rather than applying them directly. **verified** — [Palantir Foundry docs, AI FDE Overview](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ai-fde/overview). Anyone citing "FDE" from Palantir material after this docs page existed must check whether the source means the person or the agent.

### 4. Palantir's engagement entry motion, as stated to investors

Palantir's most recent Form 10-K describes the standard entry into an engagement as an unpaid or low-cost trial: "We often also provide our platforms to potential customers (including individual users at such customers) at no or low cost initially to them for evaluation purposes through short-term pilot deployments of our platforms, including at bootcamps, and there is no guarantee that we will be able to convert customers from these short-term pilot deployments to longer-term revenue-generating contracts." **verified** — [Palantir Technologies Inc., Form 10-K for FY2025, filed 2026-02-17](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1321655/000132165526000011/pltr-20251231.htm).

Worth recording as a negative finding: the phrase "forward deployed" does not appear anywhere in that 10-K, and neither does "Deployment Strategist." **verified** — full-text search of the filing during this run. The role is a hiring-and-engineering-culture construct in Palantir's public writing, not a term the company uses in its investor disclosure. Any narrative that treats FDE as Palantir's stated business model rests on the careers site and blog, not on the filings. **inferred**.

### 5. Anthropic's Forward Deployed Engineer

Anthropic's live posting places the role on the Applied AI team: "you will be a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) who embeds directly with our most strategic customers to drive transformational AI adoption. You will collaborate closely with customer teams to ship advanced AI applications that solve real world business problems." The stated deliverables are unusually concrete: "Work within customer systems to build production applications with Claude models"; "Deliver technical artifacts for customers like MCP servers, sub-agents, and agent skills that will be used in production workflows"; "Provide white glove deployment support"; "Identify and codify repeatable deployment patterns and contribute insights back to our Product and Engineering teams." The posting also says these hires "serve as one of our founding FDEs who helps to shape our forward-deployed motion" and that Anthropic expects them "to operate autonomously, thrive under ambiguity." Requirements are 4+ years in a technical customer-facing role, "production experience with LLMs including advanced prompt engineering, agent development, evaluation frameworks, and deployment at scale," and Python proficiency; travel is estimated at 25%. Annual salary is stated as $280,000–$320,000 USD. **verified** — [Anthropic, Forward Deployed Engineer (New York / San Francisco / Seattle)](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/anthropic/jobs/5302966008), posting last updated 2026-08-21. **Retrieved:** 2026-08-29.

On the retrieval date Anthropic's public board listed five Forward-Deployed-titled roles: the FDE above, FDE postings in Munich and Paris, a Manager of Forward Deployed Engineering in London, and a Pre-Sales Program Lead, Forward Deployed Engineering. **verified** — [Anthropic Greenhouse board API](https://boards-api.greenhouse.io/v1/boards/anthropic/jobs?content=true). **Retrieved:** 2026-08-29.

### 6. How Anthropic describes the engagement around the FDE

The two non-IC postings are the more informative sources on delivery method, because they describe the seams the engineer works inside.

The manager posting states that the FDE manager will "work hand-in-hand with Engagement Managers who own delivery logistics and stakeholder management," and lists as a responsibility: "Collaborate with account teams and Engagement Managers during the pre-sales process to qualify engagements, scope work, and inform statements of work." It also asks the manager to "build repeatable playbooks, starter repositories, integration templates, and an internal knowledge base that captures what your team learns in the field," and to "review technical architectures and code produced by your FDEs." Travel is 25–50%, "particularly during engagement kickoffs." The posting is candid that the method is not yet settled: "You'll be defining what good looks like for FDE management at Anthropic — there is no existing playbook to follow." **verified** — [Anthropic, Manager, Forward Deployed Engineering, London](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/anthropic/jobs/5385634008), last updated 2026-08-21. **Retrieved:** 2026-08-29.

The pre-sales posting names the lifecycle boundary directly. It describes building "the pursuit-to-signature process (including the legal and finance ways of working) that gets contracts signed quickly and cleanly," and says the holder "will work hand in glove with a Post-Sale Program Lead counterpart who owns delivery from signature onward. Together you'll define the handoff that connects your two halves of the engagement lifecycle." It further asks for "the prioritization heuristics that decide where a capacity-constrained team places its bets across commercial, research, and mission-oriented deals," and for partner motions covering "when we pass an opportunity to a partner, when we team, and how we run subcontract or co-delivery pursuits." **verified** — [Anthropic, Pre-Sales Program Lead, Forward Deployed Engineering](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/anthropic/jobs/5391012008), last updated 2026-08-21. **Retrieved:** 2026-08-29.

Anthropic's own announcements show a second delivery channel in which the forward-deployed engineer is not an Anthropic employee at all. The DXC alliance announcement describes FDEs as "engineers embedded directly inside customer organizations" and says DXC will train "tens of thousands" of Claude-certified FDEs drawn from its own development teams and certified through Anthropic Academy. **verified** — [Anthropic, "DXC integrates Claude into systems regulated industries rely on", 2026-06-11](https://www.anthropic.com/news/dxc-anthropic-alliance). A separate announcement states that Anthropic is "scaling our partner-facing team fivefold, so that we can provide dedicated Applied AI engineers to partners working on live customer deals, technical architects to scope more complex implementations, and localized go-to-market support." **verified** — [Anthropic, "Anthropic invests $100 million into the Claude Partner Network", 2026-03-12](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-partner-network); note that this second page describes Applied AI engineers and technical architects and does not use the FDE title.

### 7. OpenAI: two distinct forward-deployed titles

OpenAI's official job board distinguishes a Forward Deployed Engineer from a Forward Deployed Software Engineer, and the difference is one of ownership versus reusability.

The FDE posting states: "Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) lead complex end-to-end deployments of frontier models in production alongside our most strategic customers. You will own discovery, technical scoping, system design, build, and production rollout, partnering directly with customer engineering and domain teams." It defines success explicitly: "You will measure success through production adoption, measurable workflow impact, and eval-driven feedback that changes product and model roadmaps." Listed responsibilities include "Own technical delivery across multiple deployments from first prototype to stable production," "Make trade-offs between scope, speed, and quality," "Contribute directly in the code when progress or clarity depends on it," and "Codify working patterns into tools, playbooks, or building blocks that others can use." It asks for 5+ years, hybrid work in San Francisco, and "travel up to 50%." Compensation tier shown is $162K–$280K plus equity. **verified** — [OpenAI, Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) – SF](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/openai/305a4b22-7ff9-4fa5-9229-c6a22c9aa64f), published 2025-08-06. **Retrieved:** 2026-08-29.

The FDSWE posting describes the same team from the platform side: "As an FDSWE, you will work with our customers and OpenAI Forward Deployed Engineers to design and implement scalable solutions… You will design abstractions to solve customer problems, and then use them to scale our speed and quality of delivery across all Forward Deployed engagements." It also names the scoping artefact — "Prepare detailed scopes of work and project plans for both proof-of-concept prototypes and full production deployments" — and the working posture: "coding side-by-side to drive projects to completion on their infrastructure." It asks for 7+ years of full-stack experience; compensation tier shown is $185K–$325K plus equity. **verified** — [OpenAI, Forward Deployed Software Engineer – SF](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/openai/00207abc-49b7-465c-a219-f7c1140f8047), published 2025-11-15. **Retrieved:** 2026-08-29.

Both postings open with the same team description: "OpenAI's Forward Deployed Engineering team partners with customers to turn research breakthroughs into production systems. We operate at the intersection of customer delivery and core platform development." On the retrieval date OpenAI's board carried 18 Forward-Deployed-titled postings across San Francisco, New York, Seattle, Washington DC, Tokyo, Seoul, Singapore, and Sydney, including sector-specific FDE roles for Healthcare and Legal. **verified** — [OpenAI job board API](https://api.ashbyhq.com/posting-api/job-board/openai?includeCompensation=true). **Retrieved:** 2026-08-29.

### 8. OpenAI's stated delivery method

OpenAI's forward-deployed engineering site gives a compact method statement: "Forward deployed engineering (FDE) is how OpenAI brings AI into production for complex, real-world use cases. Instead of starting with a general product, FDE teams work directly with customers to solve a specific problem, validate impact, and then identify patterns that can scale." It lists four operating principles — "Build from first principles," "Prioritize speed and real-world impact," "Work directly with domain experts," "Deliver early value, then iterate toward scale" — and names the feedback loop as "build, prove, generalize," through which field work "identif[ies] repeatable patterns that evolve into product capabilities." **verified** — [OpenAI, "Forward deployed engineering at OpenAI"](https://openai.com/business/the-openai-deployment-company/) (resolves to deploy.co). **Retrieved:** 2026-08-29.

The launch announcement for the OpenAI Deployment Company is the most explicit first-party description of engagement shape found in this run: "A typical OpenAI Deployment Company engagement will begin with a focused diagnostic of where AI can create the most value, followed by a small number of priority workflows selected with the customer's leadership and operating teams. The OpenAI Deployment Company FDEs will then work inside the organization to design, build, test, and deploy production systems, connecting OpenAI models to the customer's data, tools, controls, and business processes so teams can use them reliably in day-to-day work." The same announcement describes the FDE mandate as working "closely with business leaders, operators, and frontline teams to identify where AI can make the biggest impact, redesign organizational infrastructure and critical workflows around it, and turn those gains into durable systems," and states that OpenAI has agreed to acquire the consulting firm Tomoro, bringing "approximately 150 experienced Forward Deployed Engineers and Deployment Specialists" on day one. **verified** — [OpenAI, "OpenAI launches the OpenAI Deployment Company", 2026-05-11](https://openai.com/index/openai-launches-the-deployment-company/). The acquisition is stated as "subject to customary closing conditions… expected to close in the coming months," so headcount and structure are announced intent, not confirmed fact.

### 9. Where the sources disagree

These are not one job under three logos, and the memo should not blend them.

**The artefact differs.** Palantir's FDSE configures and extends Palantir's own platforms — "we are actually deploying existing software products to achieve the customer's outcomes" (Dev versus Delta). OpenAI's and Anthropic's FDEs build inside the *customer's* systems: "Work within customer systems to build production applications with Claude models" (Anthropic), "connecting OpenAI models to the customer's data, tools, controls, and business processes" (OpenAI). **verified** by quotation; the contrast is **inferred**.

**The seniority and price differ by a wide margin.** Palantir's FDSE asks 1+ years at $135K–$200K; OpenAI's FDE asks 5+ years at $162K–$280K and its FDSWE 7+ years at $185K–$325K; Anthropic's FDE asks 4+ years at $280K–$320K. **verified** from the four postings. A plausible reading is that Palantir's platform absorbs the difficulty that the AI labs still ask the individual to absorb — but that is **inferred** from the JDs and is not something any of the three states.

**Travel expectations differ threefold.** 25% at Palantir and Anthropic, "up to 50%" at OpenAI, 25–75% for Palantir's Deployment Strategist. **verified.**

**Only Anthropic's postings name the commercial lifecycle boundary explicitly** (pre-sales qualification, statement of work, signature, a Post-Sale Program Lead who "owns delivery from signature onward"). Neither Palantir's nor OpenAI's engineer-facing postings describe where authority passes from sales to delivery. **verified** as a negative finding across the postings read in this run; absence in four postings is not proof the boundary is undefined at those companies. **inferred.**

**None of the primary sources states when an FDE stops writing code or hands the system off to the customer's own staff.** The closest first-party statements are Palantir's separate Enablement Engineer role for post-deployment support and 24/7 incident response, Anthropic's separate Engagement Manager and Post-Sale Program Lead functions, and OpenAI's "deliver early value, then iterate toward scale." An exit condition is nowhere written down in the material read here. **verified** as a gap.

### 10. Secondary sources, clearly labelled

Latent Space published a long-form piece on forward deployed engineering featuring Natalie Meurer, Head of Agent Engineering at Sierra, dated 2026-07-01. Meurer says Sierra's discovery aims "to find the intersection between problems that are genuinely difficult… and problems that will have a meaningful business impact," and that "most customer-specific work takes place at the orchestration layer rather than in the models themselves." Asked whether Palantir's model influenced Sierra's, she says it did "somewhat," but that Sierra "intentionally called the role agent engineer, rather than forward deployed engineer." **secondary** — [Latent Space, "Forward Deployed Engineers and the future of software engineering", 2026-07-01](https://www.latent.space/p/forward-deployed-engineers-aiewf); this is an interview write-up, not Sierra's own careers or engineering documentation, and Sierra's first-party material was not checked in this run.

## Deliberate boundaries

- This memo is a record of what three companies say about a job title. It is not a definition adopted by this repository. `CONTEXT.md` already defines **FDE** for this project's purposes, and nothing here amends it.
- The commercial machinery quoted above — pre-sales qualification, statements of work, deal prioritisation, partner subcontracting — is recorded because it is what the primary sources actually describe. It is out of scope for this repository's governed-operating-capability focus, consistent with the boundary already stated in [`fde-guidance-book-review.md`](./fde-guidance-book-review.md).
- Job postings are hiring artefacts written to attract candidates. They are the most specific public statement of a capability list, and they are also marketing. Treat responsibility lists as claims about intended scope, not as evidence of observed practice.
- Salary ranges, headcounts, and posting counts are single-day snapshots and should be re-checked, not cited forward.

## What could not be verified

- **Palantir's Medium blog is inaccessible to plain HTTP fetching** (HTTP 403 to both WebFetch and curl). The two Palantir blog posts cited here were read through a scripted browser during this run; a later reader may hit the same 403. Their content is quoted rather than paraphrased for that reason.
- **openai.com is likewise 403 to plain fetching**; the OpenAI announcement and forward-deployed-engineering pages were also read through a scripted browser.
- **No Palantir first-party page describing the bootcamp engagement was retrievable.** `palantir.com/bootcamps/` and `palantir.com/bootcamp/` returned only a title and sitemap link. The only bootcamp evidence in this memo is the single 10-K sentence, which is a risk-factor disclosure, not a method description.
- **Anthropic's "Forward Deployed Engineer, Federal Civilian" posting (Greenhouse job 5079562008) appeared in search results but is no longer on Anthropic's live board** as of the retrieval date. It was not read and is not cited. This is a concrete illustration of postings expiring.
- **No first-party Anthropic or OpenAI engineering-blog post describing FDE delivery methodology was found.** For Anthropic, the methodology evidence is entirely job postings plus partnership announcements; there is no equivalent of Palantir's Dev-versus-Delta essay.
- **No primary source states an FDE exit or handoff criterion.** Claims that the model includes a defined handoff should be treated as unsupported until such a source is produced.
- **No X/Twitter post is cited in this memo.** Search surfaced a large volume of FDE commentary on aggregator and interview-prep sites (Exponent, fde.academy, Paraform and similar); none of it was used, because in every case the underlying primary document was reachable directly.
- **The Tomoro acquisition and the ~150-FDE figure are announced intent**, subject to regulatory approval per OpenAI's own text. Do not cite them as completed.
